Binary Systems


If you've ever seen a sci-fi movie (or any movie related to tech, for that matter), you've probably seen something like this before: a seemingly random string of 1s and 0s. But what do they mean?

Binary code consists out of 2 digits: 0 (which means "off") and 1 (which means "on"). A single 0 or 1 makes a bit, which is puny on its own, but in a string of 8 total, what's called a byte, they can create 256 combinations of a string.

Binary code equates to numbers. Those numbers are calculated with either a single one and/or powers of two, as can be seen in the second image. The more left of the first binary decimal, the greater the power of two. A 0 would mean the absence of the number; a 1 the presence. By summation you can add the numbers up and form an entire numbers, up to the number 255 in bytes, meaning 256 combinations (including 0) as stated prior. Those entire numbers can dictate which text character is displayed or which colour a pixel can be, among much more.
